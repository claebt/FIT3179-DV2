Why We Love the Coast

A data story about what makes a great Australian beach, and how to find yours.

Australia's beaches are a national obsession. This story looks at their scale, how often we use them, what's on offer at them, and how to enjoy them safely.

    A coastline like no other

    Western Australia alone has more coastline than most countries

    Australia's coastline is spread very unevenly. Western Australia, Queensland and the Northern Territory hold most of it, while New South Wales, the most populous state, has one of the shortest.

    How to read: darker blue means more coastline. Hover over a state for its mainland and island split, or over an orange dot for one of 2026's best beaches.

    Key takeaway: Western Australia's 20,788 km is about 35% of the national total, and almost 40% of Australia's coastline is around islands.

    Source: Geoscience Australia, Border Lengths - States and Territories; Tourism Australia, Australia's Best Beaches 2026; ABS, ASGS Edition 3 State and Territory boundaries.

    Where we go and what we do

    The coast is part of everyday Australian life

    87% of Australians live within 50 km of the coast, and more than 16 million adults made over 630 million coastal visits in a single year.

    88 in every 100 coastal visits are to a beach

    When Australians head to the coast, they overwhelmingly head to the sand.

    How to read: each square is 1% of coastal visits.

    Key takeaway: for most people, "the coast" means the beach.

    Swimming is by far the most popular coastal activity

    More than half of adults swim at the coast, but most only go occasionally.

    How to read: dark blue is at least once a month, light blue is less often.

    Key takeaway: surfing and rock fishing are the only activities where regulars outnumber occasional participants.

    Source: Surf Life Saving Australia, National Coastal Safety Report 2025.

    5. Is the water clean?

    Ocean beaches nearly always pass, but sheltered waters don't

    Beach water quality is graded from Very Good to Very Poor. In 2026, 97% of monitored NSW ocean beaches were Good or Very Good, but only 79% of all swim sites were. Estuaries, lakes, lagoons and rivers did worse because they flush more slowly after rain.

    Bacteria levels at Sydney swim sites

    6. Enjoying it safely

    Every coastal drowning last year happened away from the flags and patrols

    Last year 154 people drowned on Australia's coast, the highest number on record. The national report found that none of those deaths happened between the red and yellow flags while a patrol was on duty.

    Coastal drowning deaths by state, 2024/25

    Each state is resized to match its number of deaths: a state with twice the deaths covers twice the area. Colour shows the rate per 100,000 people.

    How to read: small states have fewer deaths partly because fewer people live there, so compare colours for risk, not sizes. States are moved closer together to save space. Hover for exact figures.
